# Design Partner Interview Guide

## Goal

Find two equipment sellers who have recurring financing demand and are willing to test real applications inside their current sales process.

The interview is not a pitch survey. Positive opinions do not validate the model. We need evidence from actual quotations, lost deals and workflow constraints.

## Target respondent

Prefer one of:

- owner;
- commercial director;
- head of sales;
- sales-operations owner;
- finance/leasing coordinator.

A salesperson without process authority can provide discovery data but cannot qualify the company as a design partner.

## Pre-interview data

Record only public/company-level information:

- company name and website;
- equipment category;
- geography;
- apparent price range;
- sales channels;
- B2B focus;
- existing finance/leasing messaging;
- decision-maker role.

Personal contact details must be stored privately, not in this repository.

## Interview script — 35 minutes

### 1. Current sales flow — 5 minutes

- How does a typical B2B deal start?
- At what point is a quotation created?
- Who knows the final equipment price and delivery terms?
- How long is the sales cycle?
- What percentage of quotations become sales?

### 2. Financing demand — 8 minutes

- How often do customers ask for leasing, instalments or financing?
- In how many recent cases did budget/advance payment stop the purchase?
- What ticket range generates financing demand?
- Which client types ask most often?
- Does demand differ for new and used equipment?
- Can you recall three real recent examples?

### 3. Existing solution — 7 minutes

- Which lessors/banks do you currently recommend?
- How is an application transferred?
- Who follows the status?
- How quickly does the client receive a meaningful response?
- What information is repeatedly requested?
- How often does the client disappear between seller and lessor?
- Can you tell why an application was rejected?
- Do you receive remuneration, and can you reconcile it?

### 4. Embedded path — 8 minutes

Show only the process, not a polished product:

1. co-branded link or assisted form;
2. client confirms contact and consent;
3. seller's quotation context is attached;
4. Finuslugi qualifies and routes;
5. seller sees non-sensitive status;
6. advertiser completes the transaction;
7. partner reward is calculated after the cash event.

Ask:

- Where should this appear in your current process?
- Who would initiate it?
- Which fields are already available in the quotation?
- What would stop managers from using it?
- Which statuses are useful without violating client confidentiality?
- What response time is required to protect the equipment sale?
- Would you test it with real clients during the next 30 days?

### 5. Commercial and operational commitment — 5 minutes

- How many relevant applications could realistically be tested?
- Who will own the pilot internally?
- Can we compare pilot outcomes with the current process?
- Are you willing to add a co-branded link/form?
- Are you willing to provide outcome feedback?
- What partner-reward model is understandable: fixed CPA, share of received commission or hybrid?

### 6. Closing evidence — 2 minutes

Ask for one concrete next action:

- nominate a process owner;
- share an anonymized quotation template;
- schedule workflow mapping;
- approve a test link placement;
- identify the first eligible real request.

No next action means the company remains an interview participant, not a design partner.

## Evidence questions that resist polite answers

Instead of:

> Would this be useful?

Ask:

> In the last 30 days, how many quotations above the relevant ticket did not close because the customer lacked budget or needed financing?

Instead of:

> Would managers use the form?

Ask:

> Which exact screen, document or message would contain the link, and who is responsible for inserting it?

Instead of:

> Would you become a partner?

Ask:

> Which real customer case can we process first, and when is the next quotation likely to be issued?

## Design-partner scorecard

Score 0–5.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Monthly relevant quotation volume | 15 |
| Demonstrated financing demand | 15 |
| Ticket supports economics | 10 |
| Decision-maker involvement | 10 |
| Willingness to run real cases | 15 |
| Ability to embed workflow | 10 |
| Status feedback discipline | 8 |
| Data/consent discipline | 7 |
| Segment fit with advertisers | 5 |
| Access and trust advantage | 5 |

Maximum: 100.

## Design-partner qualification

A company becomes a design partner only when:

- score is at least 70;
- an internal owner is named;
- at least one real eligible request is expected;
- the test path and responsibilities are agreed;
- no payment or approval promise is made to the client;
- the partner accepts source tracking and status reconciliation;
- legal/compliance review does not prohibit the scenario.

## Pilot expectations

The design partner receives:

- co-branded application path;
- onboarding and manager script;
- agreed status visibility;
- manual support during pilot;
- transparent reward rule after the cash event;
- pilot review with outcome data.

Finuslugi receives:

- real high-intent applications;
- quotation context;
- process feedback;
- status verification;
- permission to measure outcomes;
- no public endorsement unless separately approved.

## Stop signals

Reject or pause the partner when:

- requested applications are invented or sent without client awareness;
- managers promise approval or a specific rate;
- source attribution is intentionally bypassed;
- the company demands payment for raw contact upload;
- contact data is shared outside the approved path;
- the company refuses feedback but expects commission;
- one partner would create unacceptable concentration;
- the ticket or frequency cannot support positive cash contribution.
